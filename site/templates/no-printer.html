The card path

If you own a notebook, you already own the good part.

What you come back with is the thing you have been going round and round on, decided. Not narrowed to three options and handed back to you: decided, in your own handwriting, in a notebook you already own, somewhere with nothing waiting on you.

You say one sentence. The session arrives as a card: one line naming what you are really deciding, one prompt per page, a few numbered handles where the AI has done work worth reacting to. You copy that part into the notebook by hand, take it away from the desk, and answer it in pen for as long as it takes. Photograph the pages afterwards and hand them back. What you wrote outranks anything proposed before you picked up the pen.

A notebook is what every sheet in this project labours to become: a mostly empty page where your pen is the highest-contrast thing on it and nothing printed argues with it. You have one already.

The sentence

Your AI is not allowed to ask you this, so you get to say it first.

Can you print, can you read the page: both questions are banned outright in the reference the skill loads before it designs anything. So the card path only ever answers something you said. Add this to whatever you were already asking for, or send it on its own after a PDF has turned up.

I can’t print anything today. Dictate this as a card I can copy into the notebook I already have.

Or select the sentence and copy it.

Scope

If the reason is that a printed page is not how you read, there is a sentence written for that further down. Both reasons reach the same place: the session in plain lines, which is what a notebook wants and equally what a screen reader, a braille slate, a typed file, a voice memo or somebody writing at your direction wants. What you do with the lines is yours.

Ask for the PDF later, for a colleague or for someone writing at your direction, and it is rendered without comment. It is never pushed at you unasked.

What arrives

The card comes in two parts, and you copy one of them.

That split is the whole format, and it exists to keep the writing you do worth doing. One part goes into the notebook in your hand. The other is read once on screen and never copied: structure, house rules, the pen legend, everything that would otherwise cost you a paragraph of transcription and buy you no thinking.

How much there is to copy is given to you as a number of words before you start, so you know what you are agreeing to. Never as a number of minutes: nothing in this system will tell you how long your own thinking ought to take.

Worth knowing before you lean on it

A pasted protocol is ordinary user text sitting underneath whatever the product has already told the model, so every rule in it is a request. A host tuned to be maximally helpful can still pre-fill a zone the rules say to leave empty, or volunteer a duration the rules ban. How often, and on which product, nobody has measured. The ledger says that in those words rather than rounding it up.

If a printed page is not how you read

The same card is the whole session, and the return trip is built to match it.

Say so and the session is dictated: plain lines you can work through a slate, a keyboard, a voice memo, or somebody writing at your direction. Dictated, typed and scribed pages come back carrying exactly the authority handwriting carries, and a scribe is never read as a second participant.

What that costs is stated in the repository rather than summarised here, because a summary is where a claim like this gets rounded up. It is not the same session, and nothing on this site is allowed to imply that it is.

I can’t read a printed sheet. Dictate this session as a card in plain lines I can work from.

Where the evidence stops

Everything behind the printed sheet was measured on someone looking at a page.

So the brief opens a heading for the unprinted page, puts no sources under it, and records cluster by cluster what stops applying. Two things there are worth carrying with you.

Copying a card out by hand may change how you hold the problem, and nothing this project prints or says may tell you that it does: no study has tested a hand-copied scaffold, and a plausible mechanism is not a finding. The second is blunter. The copy is where people are most likely to stop, and the ones who finish are predicted to rate it poorly. That is said in advance rather than after, and it is not an argument against doing it. The heading is below in full.

The page still comes back

A notebook spread is read as carefully as a scan of a printed sheet.

Photograph it and hand it over. A bound notebook usually photographs as a spread, one image holding two pages, and it is read as the two pages it is. With no printed layer there is no printed ink key to consult, so your marks resolve against a legend you wrote on the page and otherwise against the defaults. One rung fewer in the cascade, and nothing else changes.
