You installed it and then nothing happened

You don’t have to know the format. You only have to say what is true.

Twelve opening lines below. Each one is a precondition — a description of the situation you are actually in — rather than an instruction. Copy the one that is true, put your own specifics in the blanks, and let the skill choose the kit.

Why a precondition and not a command?

Because several of the named session formats will not print without one, and the honest way to satisfy a gate is to state the thing yourself rather than be interviewed about it. An after-action review needs a plan you recorded before you started and a log of what actually happened. A weekly review needs an inventory of open loops your assistant can genuinely see. Brainwriting rounds need three to six people in one room. Say the condition in your own words and the gate is answered in the same breath as the request.

The reverse also holds, and it is a feature: if the condition isn’t true, the format should decline. A sheet that prints anyway — a reference class it invented, an inventory of loops it guessed at — is worse than no sheet.

Twelve lines

Grouped by the situation, because that is the part you already know.

Everything set in serif below is yours to type. Square brackets are blanks — replace them before you send. Read the note under a line before you copy it; some of these formats are wrong for some people, and that is stated on purpose.

Something just ended.

  1. Line 1 · After-action review · Deep, 2–3 pages

    The [spring launch] is finished. I have the plan we wrote down before we started and the log of what actually happened, both in this thread. Give me an after-action review on paper.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    No record, no review. The recorded plan and the execution log are a hard condition rather than decoration: written reflection with no objective record to react to is the version that produces no improvement at all. If you have only your memory of it, take line 2 instead.

    Nothing gets ranked on this kit, and it is not the sheet for emotional processing after something distressing — that is line 2 as well.

  2. Line 2 · Distanced pair · Deep, its own sheet

    Something about [the handover] went badly and I want to sit with it rather than diagnose it. Give me a reflection sheet, not a review.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    Two zones: what happened and what you make of it, then what it felt like — both, because writing about thoughts and feelings together beats either alone. The prompts are written at arm’s length, in the second person, because that distance is the working part. It keeps its own sheet: no ranking, no planning, nothing to prioritise beside it.

Something is about to be committed.

  1. Line 3 · Premortem · Deep, 2 pages

    The [migration plan] is committed and I’m confident in it. It’s a year from now and it has failed completely. Print me the premortem.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    The premortem exists to deflate overconfidence, and every study behind it targets exactly that. If you are already lying awake about this plan, there is nothing here to deflate and no evidence that deflating the deflated helps — take line 4, or line 2. It also does not want a repeat run at an unchanged plan.

    The kit deliberately prints no countermeasures. Naming the ways it fails and then fixing them are separate events; the fixing happens on screen after you scan back.

  2. Line 4 · Outside view · Deep, 2 pages

    I’m about to commit to [a date / a budget / an adoption number]. There are comparable past cases, some of them ours. Give me the outside view before I sign up to it.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    The kit prints a reference class with its provenance stated on the page, and your own past cases beat strangers’ whenever you have them. If no defensible reference class exists, or the base rates cannot be sourced out loud, the format is supposed to decline: a fabricated distribution printed as an anchor is worse than no sheet.

    What it promises is calibration, never accuracy. It removes systematic optimism; it does not sharpen any individual estimate.

Something was just explained to you at length.

  1. Line 5 · Teach-back sheet · Deep, 2 pages, closed book

    You’ve spent this whole session explaining [how the auth flow works] and I stayed with all of it. Close the book — give me the teach-back sheet.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    Only if you genuinely engaged with the explanation. A closed-book prompt after a skim measurably hurts, because nothing is loaded to retrieve. Say so honestly and take a different line if you were really only skimming.

    The sheet withholds every scrap of the explanation you just read, including the parts the machine obviously still has. That withholding is the instrument: print the content and a calibration test becomes a recognition test. Gap marks are annotation, never a score. This is the one place where scan-back checks your handwriting against the source instead of simply taking it as true — it is authoritative about what you believe, not about the subject.

  1. Line 6 · Weekly review · Deep, 2 pages, recurring

    It’s the top of my week. Inventory the open loops you can actually see — the threads here, [my task list, my calendar] — and print the weekly review.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    Only what it can genuinely see. If your assistant has no real view of your threads, tasks or calendar, the format should be skipped rather than filled with guesses — a page of invented loops is design fixation in its purest form, and the second page, the mind-sweep of what has your attention that no system knows about, is never pre-filled at all.

    No quadrants, no priority matrix, no scoring of any kind. Weekly, not daily. Next actions get drafted after you scan it back, not on the sheet.

  2. Line 7 · Light sheet · exactly one page

    It’s the evening and I’m on the couch. Give me a Light sheet on [the backlog] — things to react to, circle and cross out. One page.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    Light means one page, and one pen gesture per item: circle, rank, strike, a single word. It is a capacity, not a difficulty setting — a Deep provocation printed on a Light sheet simply does not get done, which is why naming your capacity up front is worth a clause.

You have to make something that isn’t there yet.

  1. Line 8 · Long list, with Spent Ground · Deep

    I need [twenty names for this thing] and everything I’ve come up with so far is the obvious version. Name the obvious territories as already-walked ground, then leave the rest of the page to me.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    “Territories” is doing real work in that sentence. The pattern names the obvious ground as abstract categories, never as examples: categories roughly double creative output, while listing specific solutions and asking you to avoid them increases fixation instead. Expect ten to fourteen numbered lines rather than four — the common categories get used up in the first third, and the back half is where the odd ones live.

  2. Line 9 · One provocation per page · Deep, one question per page

    There are [three] questions here I keep deferring. Put one at the top of each page and nothing else on the page.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    The whole kit is the questions and the emptiness underneath them. It is also the format that survives being dictated into a notebook completely intact, so it is the one to ask for if you have no printer.

  3. Line 10 · Cut-apart cards · Deep, sequencing

    I have [eleven studio sessions] and the order is the problem, not the pieces. Print them as cards I can cut apart and move around a table.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    Cards earn their place when the arrangement is genuinely hard to hold in your head: many items, spatial or relational. Moving things with your hands is not a general creativity booster — controlled studies find no such effect — so a four-item list does not qualify. Ask for cards when the arrangement is the problem. The set comes with blank cards, because without them it reads as closed.

You are not alone, or not at a desk.

  1. Line 11 · Brainwriting rounds · Deep, group only

    There will be [five] of us in one room on [Thursday] with one problem to open up: [the onboarding drop-off]. Print one brainwriting sheet per person.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    Group only: three to six people, in the same room, passing sheets by hand. Never solo — the solo variant was evaluated and cut, because re-anchoring you on your own first ideas may reinstate the fixation the pass was relieving. The page cap counts per participant, so five people is one page pattern printed five times, not a five-page kit.

    Nothing on the sheet is a timer and no round count is promised; the passing is event-based — when your three are down, pass left. The name box is optional and leaving it blank is fully supported. Choosing a winner happens afterwards, on a separate sheet.

  2. Line 12 · Grinnell field kit · Deep series, one kit per sitting

    Over the next several days I’ll be [running these interviews], and the same [three teams] keep coming up. Set me up with a Grinnell field kit, one per sitting.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Scope

    A series, not a sheet: one kit per sitting, up to four pages each, with an account page per recurring entity that accumulates across days. More entities means more sessions, not more pages. On the return trip, scan-back appends to each entity’s running account and reorders by the dates in your own handwriting.

    It is a journal in the fact-forward register, not a diary — the reflective voice belongs on line 2 and never merges with this. If nothing actually recurs, ask for a plain observation log instead.

One format is deliberately not on this page

The serial disclosure kit has a gate a copy button cannot carry.

It is three sittings of expressive writing on consecutive days — the only pages in the whole system that print private and never come back — closed by a fourth page, the distillation, which is the one that does return. The offer itself has to do things a button cannot do: say out loud, before you agree, that the first sittings reliably feel worse and that the dip is unrelated to whether the thing works; promise no relief; and propose the whole series rather than a page, because the effect is small, delayed, and accrues across sittings.

It is for work friction and professional setbacks only, never therapy, treatment or clinical support, and it is ruled out entirely for anyone in acute distress, after recent or severe trauma, or seeking trauma processing. All of that is a conversation with something that can hear your answer. A copy button hears nothing, so there is nothing here to copy. If it sounds like your situation, describe the situation in your own words and let the skill make the offer properly — including the parts you would rather it skipped.

One more sentence, and it is not a session

If you can’t print, you have to be the one to say so.

The skill never asks whether you have a printer and never asks whether you can read the sheet. Both questions are banned outright, so the card path only ever answers something you said first. Add this to any line above, or send it on its own after a PDF has already appeared.

I can’t print anything today. Dictate this as a card I can copy into the notebook I already have.

Scope

You get the prompts and the structure, never the machine’s lists. Some patterns cross to a notebook intact, some cross only as numbered handles, and some do not cross at all. The full account is on the no-printer path.
