You installed it and then nothing happened

Twelve sentences. Copy whichever one sounds like your week.

There is no vocabulary to learn first. Each line below describes a situation somebody is actually in. If one of them is you, put your own specifics where the brackets are and send it. A page comes out, you go and fill it in somewhere away from the screen, and what you wrote by hand is what the next stretch of work gets built on.

Twelve lines

Grouped by what is going on, not by what the page is called.

Everything set in serif is yours to type. Square brackets are blanks, so swap your own words in before you send. A few of these are wrong for some people, and where that is true it says so under the line. That part is worth reading before you copy.

Something just ended.

  1. Line 1 · After-action review · two or three pages

    You come back with an honest account of why it went the way it did, written while you still remember. That includes the version people almost never write: what went wrong inside something that worked.

    The [spring launch] is finished. I have the plan we wrote down before we started and the log of what actually happened, both in this thread. Give me an after-action review on paper.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    One condition

    It needs a record. The plan as written down beforehand and the log of what happened both have to exist, because reflection with nothing objective to push against does not improve anything. If all you have is your memory of it, line 2 is the better one. And if what you want is to sit with something that hurt rather than diagnose it, that is line 2 as well. This page is not built for that.

  2. Line 2 · Distanced pair · one sheet, on its own

    The thing you have been carrying around about it finally gets put down, in your own handwriting, far enough back from it that you can read it again later.

    Something about [the handover] went badly and I want to sit with it rather than diagnose it. Give me a reflection sheet, not a review.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    What is on it

    Two zones: what happened and what you make of it, then what it felt like. Both, because writing about the thinking and the feeling together does more than either alone. The prompts address you as “you” rather than “I”. It reads oddly at first, and then turns out to be the part that makes honesty possible. It keeps its own sheet, with nothing to rank or plan beside it.

You are about to commit to something.

  1. Line 3 · Premortem · two pages

    Six to eight ways this actually fails, found by the one person who knows the plan well enough to find them and is, for one page, not obliged to be optimistic about it.

    The [migration plan] is committed and I’m confident in it. It’s a year from now and it has failed completely. Print me the premortem.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    This one exists to puncture confidence, and every study behind it was run on people who felt sure. If you are already lying awake about this plan there is nothing left to puncture, and no evidence that puncturing the punctured helps anyone. Take line 4, or line 2. It is also not a second run at a plan you have not changed.

    The page prints no fixes on purpose. Naming how it fails and deciding what to do about it are two different sittings, and the second one happens on screen after you scan the first one back.

  2. Line 4 · Outside view · two pages

    A number you would defend out loud, and a written account of what would have to be true for you to beat what usually happens to projects like this one.

    I’m about to commit to [a date / a budget / an adoption number]. There are comparable past cases, some of them ours. Give me the outside view before I sign up to it.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    What is on it, and what it will refuse

    Comparable past cases, printed with where they came from stated on the page. Your own past cases beat strangers’ whenever you have any. If no defensible comparison exists, or the numbers cannot be sourced out loud, the format is meant to decline rather than invent a plausible-looking spread. A made-up distribution printed as an anchor is worse than no page.

    What it gives you is calibration, not accuracy. It takes out systematic optimism. It will not sharpen any one estimate.

Something big was just explained to you.

  1. Line 5 · Teach-back sheet · two pages, closed book

    By the end you know which parts of it you can genuinely explain and which parts you were nodding along to. Those turn out not to be the same parts.

    You’ve spent this whole session explaining [how the auth flow works] and I stayed with all of it. Close the book — give me the teach-back sheet.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    Only worth doing if you really stayed with the explanation. After a skim it measurably hurts, because nothing is loaded to retrieve. Say so and take a different line; nobody is checking.

    The page holds back every scrap of what you just read, including the parts the machine obviously still has. Print the content and you are testing whether you recognise it, not whether you know it. Wherever you get stuck, the mark you make is a note to yourself and never a score. This is also the one place where scan-back checks your handwriting against the source instead of taking it as settled: it treats you as authoritative about what you believe, not about the subject.

It’s the top of the week, or the end of a long day.

  1. Line 6 · Weekly review · two pages, every week

    You come back with the week’s loose ends triaged in pen, and with the ones no system knew about, because they were only ever in your head.

    It’s the top of my week. Inventory the open loops you can actually see — the threads here, [my task list, my calendar] — and print the weekly review.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Read this before you copy it

    Only what it can genuinely see. If your assistant has no real view of your threads, tasks or calendar, it should skip this rather than fill a page with plausible guesses. An invented inventory is the exact failure the whole system is built to avoid, and the second page, the sweep of what has your attention, is never pre-filled at all.

    No quadrants, no priority matrix, no scoring of any kind. Weekly rather than daily. Next actions get drafted after you scan it back, not on the page.

  2. Line 7 · Light sheet · exactly one page

    A pile of things, reacted to. Circled, struck out, ranked, one mark each, done from the couch with the television on.

    It’s the evening and I’m on the couch. Give me a Light sheet on [the backlog] — things to react to, circle and cross out. One page.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    What Light means

    One page, and one pen gesture per item: circle, rank, strike, a single word. It describes how much you have in you right now, not how hard the work is. Saying which one you are in is worth the clause, because a page built for a cleared desk does not get filled in on a couch.

You have to make something that isn’t there yet.

  1. Line 8 · Long list, with Spent Ground · ten to fourteen numbered lines

    The strange ones. The obvious territory is already fenced off at the top of the page, so your pen has nowhere to go but past it.

    I need [twenty names for this thing] and everything I’ve come up with so far is the obvious version. Name the obvious territories as already-walked ground, then leave the rest of the page to me.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Why “territories” and not “examples”

    That word is doing real work in the sentence. What gets printed is the obvious ground as broad categories, never as actual ideas. Naming the categories roughly doubles what people come up with, while listing real solutions and asking you to steer clear of them makes fixation worse.

    Expect ten to fourteen numbered lines rather than four. The common ideas get used up in the first third, and the back half is where the odd ones live.

  2. Line 9 · One provocation per page · one question per page

    The questions you keep deferring, answered in your own hand, with nothing else on the page competing for the space.

    There are [three] questions here I keep deferring. Put one at the top of each page and nothing else on the page.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Also the one that travels

    The whole thing is the questions and the emptiness underneath them, which is why it survives being read aloud into a notebook completely intact. Ask for this one if you have no printer.

  3. Line 10 · Cut-apart cards · cards to move around a table

    You come back with the order, worked out with your hands on a table where you can see all of it at once instead of four items at a time.

    I have [eleven studio sessions] and the order is the problem, not the pieces. Print them as cards I can cut apart and move around a table.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    When cards earn it

    When the arrangement is genuinely too big to hold in your head: many items, spatial or relational. Handling things is not a general creativity booster, and controlled studies find no such effect, so a four-item list does not qualify. Ask for cards when the order is the problem. The set comes with blanks, because a closed set reads as closed.

Other people are involved, or it runs across days.

  1. Line 11 · Brainwriting rounds · one sheet per person

    Everyone in the room comes back with a full sheet, including the people who would not have got a word in. Each sheet ends up carrying three ideas from every pair of hands it passed through.

    There will be [five] of us in one room on [Thursday] with one problem to open up: [the onboarding drop-off]. Print one brainwriting sheet per person.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    Groups only

    Three to six people, in the same room, passing sheets by hand. Never on your own: the solo version was tried and cut, because putting you back in front of your own first ideas may rebuild the fixation the pass had just relieved. The page cap counts per person, so five people is one page printed five times.

    Nothing on the sheet is a timer and no number of rounds is promised. When your three are down, pass left. The name box is optional, and leaving it blank is a fully supported way to use it. Choosing a winner happens afterwards, on a different sheet.

  2. Line 12 · Grinnell field kit · a series, one kit per sitting

    You come back, day after day, with an account of each person or team that keeps coming up, longer and more specific every time. Scan them in and they are stitched into one running record apiece, ordered by the dates in your own hand.

    Over the next several days I’ll be [running these interviews], and the same [three teams] keep coming up. Set me up with a Grinnell field kit, one per sitting.

    Or select the sentence and copy it.

    A series, not a sheet

    One kit per sitting, up to four pages each, with an account page for every thing that recurs. More things to track means more sittings rather than more pages.

    It is a journal in the fact-forward voice, not a diary. The reflective register belongs on line 2 and never merges with this one. If nothing actually recurs, ask for a plain observation log instead.

The one that isn’t on this page

There is a thirteenth, and it needs a conversation rather than a button.

It is three sittings of expressive writing on consecutive days. Those three are the only pages in the whole system that print private and never come back. A fourth page, the distillation, is the one that does.

The offer has to say several things out loud before you agree to it: that the first sittings reliably feel worse, and that the dip has nothing to do with whether it is working; that no relief is promised; and that what is on offer is the whole series rather than a page, because the effect is small, delayed, and accrues across sittings.

It is for work friction and professional setbacks. It is not therapy, treatment or clinical support, and it is ruled out entirely for anyone in acute distress, after recent or severe trauma, or seeking trauma processing. Those exclusions are why there is nothing here to copy: a button cannot hear your answer, and this is a place where the answer matters. If it sounds like your situation, describe the situation in your own words and let the offer be made properly, including the parts you would rather it left out.
